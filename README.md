## 👩🏻‍💻 About Me

I am an Economics PhD candidate at Victoria University of Wellington, specialising in climate and health economics. My work examines temperature-related mortality risks in New Zealand, drawing on linked administrative records and meteorological observations.

My work involves the full data analysis process, from data extraction, cleaning and integration to statistical modelling, visualisation and communicating results. I particularly enjoy working through complex data structures, developing processing workflows and turning raw data into well-organised analytical datasets.

## 🔬 Research Interests

- Climate and health economics
- Temperature-related mortality and population vulnerability
- Administrative microdata and longitudinal data analysis
- Environmental exposure assessment
- Socioeconomic and demographic heterogeneity
- Evidence-based policy research

## 📊 Current Research

**Temperature-related mortality and population vulnerability in New Zealand**

My doctoral research investigates the relationship between environmental temperature and mortality using linked administrative and meteorological data.

Current work includes:

- Estimating mortality risks associated with heat and cold exposure relative to local optimum temperatures
- Examining differences across clinical, demographic and socioeconomic population groups
- Constructing temperature exposure datasets from weather-station observations
- Developing and maintaining data-processing and statistical analysis workflows
